# Cleaning and Analysing Raw-Material Purchasing Data (PostgreSQL)

Six SQL scripts that turn a messy purchasing extract into trustworthy spend figures: top materials per category, a missing-value audit, imputation of missing quantities with a back-test, and a clean monthly spend series.

> **All data is synthetic**, generated by `src/generate_data.py` (fixed seed). Materials, prices, volumes and rejection rates are invented. Only the *shape* of the data imitates an ERP extract (dates stored as text, missing quantities, a separate table of rejected deliveries).

## Objective

Purchasing needs to know where the money goes (which five materials dominate each category) and how that develops over time. The raw extract cannot be summed directly: dates are text in two formats, some quantities are missing, and deliveries that were rejected at goods receipt are still in the order table. The scripts clean the data inside the database and make every correction visible and measurable.

## Techniques Used

- Anti-join with `NOT EXISTS` to exclude rejected deliveries
- Nested CTEs, `RANK()` and `SUM() OVER (PARTITION BY ...)` for per-category rankings and spend shares
- Generic NULL audit of all columns at once with `to_jsonb` and `jsonb_each_text`, plus `FILTER` aggregates
- Regex-guarded date parsing of a TEXT column (`CASE` + `to_date`) that tolerates two formats
- Imputation with a cascade of CTEs and a `COALESCE` fallback chain, normalising prices for rebates before grouping, and a `quantity_source` column that keeps imputed values traceable
- Leave-one-out back-test with window functions (`(SUM() OVER - own) / (COUNT() OVER - 1)`), `percentile_cont`, `GROUP BY ROLLUP`
- `LAG()` and rolling-frame `AVG() OVER` for month-over-month change and a three-month average
- Views as the interface between analysis and export (`\copy` from views to CSV)

## Key Findings

The database holds 8,000 order lines for 80 raw materials in five categories (January 2024 to June 2026, about 12.2 million EUR in total).

- **Concentration inside categories is high.** The largest material accounts for 48 % of spend in *Feldspars & Clays* (Kaolin grade A) and for 35 % in *Oxides & Colorants* (Iron oxide grade D, 2.1 million EUR). *Oxides & Colorants* alone is half of the total spend.

  | Category | Top material | Spend (EUR) | Share of category | Price variance (EUR) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Oxides & Colorants | Iron oxide grade D | 2,118,957 | 34.5 % | +55,362 |
  | Fluxes & Borates | Lithium carbonate grade B | 492,563 | 14.4 % | -1,584 |
  | Feldspars & Clays | Kaolin grade A | 418,453 | 48.2 % | -2,270 |
  | Silicates & Quartz | Zircon silicate grade B | 169,485 | 19.9 % | +2,072 |
  | Carbonates | Strontium carbonate grade C | 103,317 | 17.2 % | +3,360 |

  Price variance is standard cost minus actual cost: positive means bought below standard.

- **Missing values are limited to one column.** 138 of 8,000 lines (1.7 %) have no quantity; all other columns are complete. 58 dates arrive in ISO format instead of `DD.MM.YYYY`.
- **Imputation works almost everywhere with the most precise rule.** 136 of the 138 missing quantities were filled from the same material, market and region; one needed the material-and-market price and one the material price. None stayed unresolved.
- **The imputation error is small, but read the number with care.** In a leave-one-out back-test on 7,512 known lines the mean absolute error was 3.0 % (median 2.6 %) and 98.5 % of estimates were within 10 %. This mostly reflects the 3.5 % price scatter built into the synthetic data; with real prices the error would differ.
- **Rejected deliveries matter, but not much.** 252 lines worth about 356,000 EUR (2.9 % of spend) belong to rejected purchase orders and are excluded from all spend figures.

## Scripts

| Script | What it does |
|---|---|
| `01_schema_and_load.sql` | Creates the four tables with the extract's types (dates as TEXT, amounts as double precision) and loads the CSVs |
| `02_top5_materials_per_category.sql` | Top five materials per category by spend, with price variance and share of category spend |
| `03_missing_values_audit.sql` | Missing values per column, where missing quantities sit, which date formats occur |
| `04_impute_missing_quantity.sql` | Builds the view `purchase_orders_clean`: parsed dates, rejected flag, imputed quantities with their source |
| `05_backtest_imputation.sql` | Leave-one-out accuracy of the imputation by category |
| `06_monthly_spend.sql` | Monthly spend per category with month-over-month change and rolling average |
| `07_export_results.sql` | Writes the result views to `output/` |

## Imputation Method

A missing quantity is `net amount / unit price`, rounded to the 25 kg pack size. The unit price comes from lines of the same material where the quantity is known, using the most specific group available:

1. same material, sourcing market and buyer region
2. same material and sourcing market
3. same material

Before averaging, every price is converted to the list price `net / (1 - rebate)`, so a 5 % contract rebate does not make two otherwise identical lines look different. The step that was used is stored in `quantity_source` (`reported`, `imputed: ...`, or `unresolved`), so later analyses can include or exclude imputed lines.

## Data

| Table | Rows | Columns |
|---|---|---|
| `purchase_orders` | 8,000 | `line_id`, `po_number`, `order_date` (TEXT), `sourcing_market`, `buyer_region`, `material_id`, `net_amount_eur`, `quantity_kg` (some NULL), `rebate`, `price_variance_eur` |
| `raw_materials` | 80 | `material_id`, `category`, `sub_category`, `material_name` |
| `rejected_deliveries` | 182 | `rejected` ('Yes'), `po_number`, `sourcing_market` |

`rejected_deliveries` joins to `purchase_orders` on `po_number` and `sourcing_market`; `buyers` is part of the schema for completeness and is not needed by the scripts.

## How to Run

From the parent folder (`sql-portfolio`), start PostgreSQL once and run the scripts:

```bash
docker compose up -d --wait
for f in purchasing-data-cleaning/sql/0*.sql; do
  docker compose exec -T -w /repo/purchasing-data-cleaning db psql -U postgres -d glaze -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1 -f "sql/$(basename "$f")"
done
```

or all projects at once with `bash run_all.sh`. Results are written to `output/`. To regenerate the data: `python src/generate_data.py` (needs `numpy` and `pandas`).

## Limitations

- Prices drift over time, so averaging them over the whole period adds error; a time-aware unit price (nearest orders in time) would be more precise.
- Missing quantities are assumed to be missing at random. If large orders were systematically more likely to lack a quantity, the imputation would be biased.
- The scripts only read dates in the two formats seen in the data; any other format becomes NULL and drops out of the monthly series (none occur in this extract).
